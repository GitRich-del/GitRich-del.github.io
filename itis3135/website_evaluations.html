Website Evaluations

Ferrari (Good-Design)

The Ferrari website is a prime example of high-end, effective web design that follows CRAP principles to create a premium user experience.

  • Contrast: Excellent use of high-resolution imagery against clean, contrasting backgrounds. The bold white and red typography stands out clearly against dark or neutral backgrounds, ensuring readability and focus.
  • Repetition: Consistent use of the Ferrari red color palette, uniform button styles, and recurring navigation patterns throughout the site. This creates a cohesive brand identity and a familiar user interface.
  • Alignment: The site utilizes a sophisticated grid system. All elements, including text, images, and interactive components, are perfectly aligned, creating a clean, professional, and organized layout.
  • Proximity: Related information is logically grouped. Specifications, pricing, and configuration options for cars are kept close together, while distinct sections are clearly separated by white space to avoid clutter.

Ling's Cars (Bad-Design)

Ling's Cars is a legendary example of "organized chaos" that intentionally ignores modern design standards, resulting in an overwhelming and visually exhausting experience.

  • Contrast: The site uses an excessive number of bright, clashing colors and flashing elements. While there is technically high contrast, it is used so inconsistently that it creates visual fatigue rather than guiding the user's attention.
  • Repetition: Instead of professional repetition, the site repeats chaotic patterns, varied fonts, and distracting GIFs. There is no sense of a unified, clean brand identity beyond the shared sense of clutter.
  • Alignment: Alignment is almost entirely ignored. Elements are scattered across the page, overlapping one another, and moving in different directions, making it nearly impossible for the user to follow a logical path.
  • Proximity: Terrible use of proximity. Vital information about car leases is buried under layers of moving graphics and unrelated text. Related elements are not grouped logically, forcing the user to hunt for what they need.
